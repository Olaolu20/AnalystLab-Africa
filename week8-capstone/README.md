# Global Poverty & Inequality Trends: A WDI Analysis

## Overview
This project applies the end-to-end data analytics workflow to examine global poverty and inequality trends using the World Bank's World Development Indicators (WDI) dataset. It was completed as the Week 8 Capstone Project for the AnalystLab Africa Data Analytics Internship (Batch A).

## Objective
To identify patterns in poverty headcount rates, income inequality (via the Gini index and income share distribution), and their relationship to economic and social indicators such as GDP per capita, unemployment, and literacy — surfacing actionable insights for policymakers and development stakeholders.

## Data Source
World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI): https://datatopics.worldbank.org/world-development-indicators/

The dataset covers 200+ countries and territories from 1960–2025. This analysis filters the full WDI dataset down to 10 indicators relevant to poverty and inequality.

## Key Findings
- Global income inequality (Gini index) has risen over time, even as average poverty rates have declined.
- Poverty remains heavily concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa and IDA-eligible countries.
- The sharpest recent increases in poverty are in conflict-affected countries (South Sudan, Yemen, Syria).
- Several countries — including China — show dramatic poverty rate declines between their earliest and latest recorded data.
- Income inequality is highest in several Latin American countries (Colombia, Panama, Brazil).
